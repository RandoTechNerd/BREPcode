Design mode help

Design mode is point-and-click CAD on top of BREPcode's code. It's in beta: most everyday sketching works, some things don't yet, and this page says which. No AI is needed for any of it.

Start here

Everything you do in Design mode is written into the model's code, and the code is what gets built. Switch to Code at any time to see exactly what a click did; switch back and carry on. Ctrl+Z outside a sketch takes back a whole step.

Your first part in six steps
  1. Press Sketch on the toolbar (or S) and pick the XY plane, or click a flat face of the model.
  2. Draw with L line, R rectangle, C circle, A arc. Each click puts a point; the view snaps to the grid and to points already there.
  3. Pick curves (click, Shift+click for more) and press a rule in the panel: Horizontal, Tangent, Equal…
  4. Press D and click a line or circle to give it a size. Click a size label to change it.
  5. Watch the panel: ✓ Fully defined means every curve is held; blue curves can still move. A sketch doesn't have to be fully defined to build.
  6. Choose Extrude (or Revolve), a distance and Join / Cut / New body, then Finish (or press E).

Sketching

A sketch is flat curves on a plane. Closed outlines become shapes you can extrude or revolve; an outline inside another is a hole.

ToolHow
Line LClick, click, click: each click ends a line and starts the next. Click the first point again to close. Esc ends the chain. A line drawn within 3° of level or plumb is made level / plumb for you.
Rectangle RTwo corners (click-click, or press and drag).
Circle CThe centre, then a point on the rim (or press and drag).
Arc AThe centre, the start, then the end. Arcs always run counter-clockwise from start to end. Ending on a point that's already there joins it.
Spline SClick the points it passes through; Enter or a double-click finishes. Click the first point again for a closed loop.
Trim TClick the piece of a line, arc, circle or spline to cut away, between whatever crosses or touches it. A circle trimmed becomes an arc. A curve nothing crosses is deleted.
Offset OClick a closed outline (or a circle) and type a distance: positive grows it, negative shrinks it.
Construction XClick a curve to make it construction (dashed): a guide that isn't part of the shape, like an axis or a reference line.
Dimension DA line (its length), two points (their distance), a circle or arc (its radius), two lines (the angle).

In the Select tool, drag a point and the rest of the sketch follows its rules. Delete removes the picked curves.

Rules (constraints)

Tangent

Pick any two of: a line, an arc, a circle, a spline (Shift+click the second), then press Tangent. Either order works.

PairWhere they touch
Line + circle or arcAnywhere. The line slides (or the circle moves) until it just touches. If the line ends at the arc, it runs on smoothly from that end. If a line stops short of the circle with a loose end, that end goes to where they touch, so a Trim can close the outline.
Circle or arc + circle or arcWhere they meet: outside each other, or one inside the other, whichever is nearer to how you drew them.
Spline + anythingAt the spline's end, which has to be on the other curve first (draw it from there, it snaps). The spline then leaves smoothly, the way the other curve was going.
The classic way to draw a slot or a link: draw two circles, draw the two lines past them, make each line tangent to each circle, then Trim the line ends and the inner sides of the circles. You get one closed outline that stays tangent when you change a radius.

Blue, white and red

Blue curves can still move: some size or position isn't set yet. White curves are fully held. The panel says how many more rules or dimensions would lock the whole sketch, or ✓ Fully defined. Moving the whole sketch counts too, so a sketch with nothing Fixed always has 3 left (two directions and a turn).

Red appears when a rule you're adding fights the ones already there, for example a line made tangent to a circle when both are pinned too far apart. The rule isn't added. Instead the panel names the rules in the way, shows them red on the sketch (hover a button to see which), and offers Remove … and add … to swap one for the new rule in one click.

Dimensions and sliders

Press D and click what to measure; type the size. Click any size label later to change it. Press ★ name in that box to make the size a named parameter: it goes to the top of the code as const width = 30; and becomes a slider, so you (or anyone you share the model with) can resize the part without opening the sketch.

A circle's radius is part of the circle itself, so it's always set. Trimming a circle into an arc keeps its radius as a dimension.

Trim, offset, construction

  • Trim cuts at every crossing and every tangent touch, so pieces that met still meet. Splines are split exactly, not re-drawn.
  • Offset keeps lines straight and arcs round (same centres), and corners meet again. Splines can't be offset exactly by any CAD program, so a spline's offset is fitted: within 0.01 mm of the true offset, which is far below what a printer or a mill can see.
  • Construction curves still take rules and dimensions; they just aren't part of the shape. Revolve can spin around a construction line.

Finishing a sketch

Pick Extrude (a distance) or Revolve (around the sketch's Y axis or a construction line), and how it meets the model: Join (added), Cut (taken away) or New body (a separate part). Finish writes it all into the code as one undo step. Finish is greyed out until the shape is a closed outline; the panel says what's open.

To change a sketch later, click it in the timeline at the bottom and choose Edit sketch. Finish rewrites it in place.

Working on the model

Outside a sketch: click a shape to pick it, then M move, R rotate, Shift+S scale with the gizmo; Q press-pull a face; F fillet and chamfer edges; H hole. The model browser on the left and the timeline at the bottom show every step, each linked to its line of code.

FAQ

I picked a circle and a line with Shift and pressed Tangent. Why won't they go tangent?

In builds before November 2026 this was a real bug: Tangent only worked for a line drawn from an arc's end, and it refused circles. It's fixed. Pick both (either order) and press Tangent.

If it still doesn't happen, the panel says why: something else holds them apart (usually a Fixed point or a dimension). The rules in the way turn red, and Remove … and add tangent fixes it in one click.

Why does Finish stay greyed out ("not a closed profile")?

An outline has to end exactly where it starts. Usually a line stops near a point instead of on it, or a curve overhangs a corner. Draw onto the point (it snaps), or Trim the overhang. A stray line that's only there as a guide can be made Construction (X).

Why did my other lines move when I added a rule?

A rule moves whatever is free to move. Pin what should stay with Fixed, or give it a dimension first; blue curves are the ones that can still move.

Can I undo inside a sketch?

Yes: Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y step through every change since the sketch opened. Cancel drops the whole sketch.

Do I need the AI assistant?

No. Everything on this page is plain clicking and typing. The assistant is an optional extra for describing a part in words.

Can I type the code instead?

Yes. A sketch is a sketch({ points, curves, constraints, dims }) call, and you can edit it by hand: a tangent is ["tangent", lineStart, lineEnd, centre, touchPoint]. While a sketch is open, though, leave the code alone: Finish would otherwise overwrite what you typed, and it tells you so.

Is the spline offset exact?

Within 0.01 mm. No CAD program can offset a spline exactly (the true offset of a spline isn't a spline), so all of them fit one; BREPcode tells you that it does.

Is this as good as Fusion or SolidWorks?

Not yet, and the next two sections say exactly where it falls short. What it does have: every click is readable code you own, works in any browser, and builds true curved surfaces (STEP-clean) when the exact kernel is used.

What doesn't work yet

  • Mid-spline tangency: a spline is tangent only at its ends.
  • Which rule to drop: when rules fight after a drag (not when adding a rule), the panel says what's off but not which one rule to drop.
  • Projected edges: a face's edges aren't brought into the sketch as references yet; snapping is to the sketch's own points and the grid.
  • Sketch on a face: the plane is stored as numbers, not as a link to the face, so moving that body later leaves the sketch where it was.
  • Driven (reference) dimensions and dimensions to an arc's edge aren't there; dimensions measure points, lines and radii.
  • Assemblies and joints aren't part of Design mode; parts are separate bodies placed by moves.
  • Text, slots, polygons and fillets inside a sketch aren't tools yet (fillet edges on the 3D model instead, with F).

Where code-CAD gets stuck

BREPcode is code-first: the text is the model, and Design mode is a careful way of writing that text by clicking. That's what makes the model shareable, diffable and scriptable, but it rubs against how traditional CAD works in a few places. We're working on each one; for now, this is where you'll feel it.

Turns, not a live session

Traditional CAD keeps one live model and every click edits it. Here each finished step is written into the code and the whole model is rebuilt from that code. Most rebuilds take a moment, but a big model can lag a beat after each step, and a step that fails to build turns red in the timeline instead of being refused up front.

Picking things by name

A fillet or a sketch on a face has to say which edge or face, in code. BREPcode names them from how the shape was made, but a big change earlier in the history (say, a different number of holes) can rename things, and a later step can then miss its target. This is the well-known "topological naming" problem; commercial CAD fights it too, with a lot more engineering behind it.

The solver

Sketch rules are solved by the BREP.io solver, which moves points a little at a time until the rules hold. It's good for everyday sketches. A very large or heavily constrained sketch can settle slowly or somewhere unexpected, where commercial solvers are faster and more predictable.

Hand edits and clicks at the same time

You can type and click on the same model, but not both at once inside an open sketch: Finish writes the sketch back as a whole, so it checks the code hasn't changed underneath it and asks you to cancel and re-open if it has.

The honest summary

For a first part, a bracket, a jig or a print-ready enclosure, Design mode should get you there. For large assemblies, heavy surfacing or production drawings, a traditional CAD program will serve you better today. We'd like to close that gap, and the list above is the order we're closing it in.
